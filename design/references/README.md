# References — things you liked

**Drop image files straight into this folder.** Screenshots, saved web pages,
photos of print, anything. Then add a block below saying what you liked about
it. That's the whole system.

A screenshot on its own is a mood board, and mood boards don't survive contact
with a real decision. The block is what makes it usable six months later.

---

## How to add one

1. Save the image into this folder. Name it anything — `stripe-login.png`,
   `IMG_4471.jpeg`, `that-green-thing.png`. Naming doesn't matter.
2. Copy the template below, fill in **at minimum** the first two lines.
3. Leave the rest blank if you don't know. Blank is fine. **Made-up is not.**

```markdown
### filename.png
**I liked** in your own words, however vague — "feels calm", "looks expensive",
             "the spacing", "I don't know, it just works"
**Because**  leave blank if you don't know why — that's normal and it's the
             reviewer's job to work out the mechanism
**Adopted**  filled in later, once something is actually decided
**Declined** filled in later, with the reason
**Status**   new / adopted / declined / partly
```

**Never edit the "I liked" line afterwards.** Those are your words and they're
the actual signal. The reviewer's interpretation goes on the "Because" line,
marked as theirs — so you can disagree with it later.

---

## Why "I don't know why" is a good answer

People point at a feeling and almost always name the wrong cause. Someone says
"I like the colour" when what they responded to was the spacing. Say the
feeling — that part is real and it's the part only you have. Working out the
mechanism is the reviewer's job, not yours.

---

## What happens to these

Before proposing anything, the Critic reads this folder. It looks for:

- **What you keep pointing at.** Three references praising the same mechanism
  is a decision waiting to be written down.
- **What you've declined.** So it never proposes it again.
- **What contradicts.** Two references pulling opposite ways is a real
  conversation, not a mistake — but somebody has to notice it first.
